Transformer Architectures: A Mixed Review of Claims
=====================================================

The Transformer architecture, first described in "Attention Is All You
Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017), has become the foundation of modern NLP.
This review examines some commonly cited but disputed claims about the
original architecture.

Regarding model size, there is significant disagreement in secondary
literature. The base model contained 65 million parameters with its
6-layer, 512-dimension configuration. However, some sources incorrectly
cite the "big" variant (which had roughly 213 million parameters using
a 1024-dimension, 16-head configuration) as the "original" model. Both
configurations were described in the same paper; the base model was used
for most ablation studies while the big model achieved the headline
BLEU scores.

On computational complexity, the standard self-attention mechanism is
unambiguously O(n²) in both time and memory with respect to sequence
length. While subsequent work (Linformer, Performer, BigBird, etc.)
introduced linear or near-linear approximations, the original 2017
Transformer did NOT include any such optimization. Claims that the
original design used sparse or approximate attention are historically
inaccurate.

The training infrastructure used 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs for the base model,
with training taking approximately 12 hours for 100,000 steps (roughly
3.5 days total). The paper used the Adam optimizer with a distinctive
warmup-then-decay learning rate schedule (warmup over 4000 steps).

Positional encoding in the original model used fixed sinusoidal functions
of different frequencies. The authors noted that learned positional
embeddings performed "nearly identically" but chose sinusoidal encodings
because they could theoretically generalize to longer sequences than
those seen during training.
