The Transformer Architecture: A Technical Overview
===================================================

The Transformer architecture was introduced in 2017 by Vaswani et al. in
their seminal paper "Attention Is All You Need," published at NeurIPS 2017.
This architecture fundamentally changed the landscape of natural language
processing by replacing recurrent computations with a purely attention-based
mechanism.

The original Transformer model described in the 2017 paper consisted of
approximately 65 million parameters in its base configuration. The model
used 6 encoder layers and 6 decoder layers, with a model dimension of 512
and 8 attention heads. The feed-forward inner dimension was set to 2048.

A key characteristic of the self-attention mechanism in Transformers is
its quadratic computational complexity — O(n²) — with respect to the
input sequence length n. This means that as the input sequence grows
longer, the computation required grows as the square of the sequence
length, since every token must attend to every other token in the
sequence. This quadratic scaling has been a major bottleneck for
processing very long documents or sequences, leading to practical limits
on context window sizes (typically 512 or 1024 tokens in early models).

The Transformer was trained on the WMT 2014 English-to-German translation
task using 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs. Training took approximately 3.5 days for
the base model. The learning rate schedule used a warmup phase followed
by inverse square root decay, which became known as the "Transformer
learning rate schedule."

The architecture uses three types of attention: encoder self-attention,
decoder self-attention (masked to prevent attending to future positions),
and encoder-decoder cross-attention. Positional encodings using sinusoidal
functions were added to give the model information about token positions,
since the attention mechanism itself is permutation-invariant.
